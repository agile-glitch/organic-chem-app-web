Disclaimer

This project is not 100 % correct and is subject to error. Its predictions, mechanisms, names, properties and suggestions come from hand-written rules and from models learned from text-mined patent data, and they can be wrong or incomplete. Use it for learning and exploration, check anything that matters against the primary literature or a qualified chemist, and do not rely on it for safety decisions or laboratory work.

Usage counts

Each browser gets a random username (shown at the bottom of the home screen). Under it, the site counts each visit and each app opened, and stores only that name, which app and the time (in a Supabase database). No IP address, browser details or anything you type or draw is stored. Clearing this site's data gives the browser a new name.

Reaction data

  • Open Reaction Database (open-reaction-database.org), CC BY-SA 4.0. The reaction knowledge base (data/rxnkb/) and the ORD reaction set (data/sources/ord.js) are derived from it and are shared under CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • US patent reactions extracted by D. M. Lowe (1976–2016), CC0.
  • Learned reactions, templates and the relevance model (data/learned/, including data/learned/m/): learned from the two reaction sets above, with templates extracted by rdchiral (MIT; C. W. Coley, W. H. Green and K. F. Jensen, J. Chem. Inf. Model. 2019, 59, 2529). They are derived from CC0 data and from the Open Reaction Database, so they are shared under CC BY-SA 4.0 with attribution to the Open Reaction Database.
  • Interferences (sheet Interferences of reaction_rules.xlsx, data/learned/i/): the patterns and advisories are written from standard organic chemistry texts (J. Clayden et al., Organic Chemistry; T. Joule and K. Mills, Heterocyclic Chemistry; P. Wuts, Greene's Protective Groups in Organic Synthesis) and the Hammett constants below; how much each matters is learned from the reaction sets above, so those weights are shared under CC BY-SA 4.0 with attribution to the Open Reaction Database.
  • Fragment Atlas (data/atlas/, data/learned/c/; tools/atlas_*.py): counted from the atom-mapped patent reactions of the USPTO-MIT set (W. Jin, C. W. Coley, R. Barzilay and T. Jaakkola, Predicting Organic Reaction Outcomes with Weisfeiler-Lehman Network, NeurIPS 2017; 479,035 reactions, derived from the D. M. Lowe patent data above, CC0), and from the recorded reactions of the learned data. The tables are counts of how often groups in recorded reactions were transformed, shared under CC BY-SA 4.0 with attribution to the Open Reaction Database for the part derived from it. The environment hashing follows the Weisfeiler-Lehman refinement of the same family of methods. Reagent and condition suggestions follow the idea of H. Gao, T. J. Struble, C. W. Coley, Y. Wang, W. H. Green and K. F. Jensen, ACS Cent. Sci. 2018, 4, 1465 (recommending reagents, solvents and catalysts from recorded reactions), here by counting what was recorded rather than by a neural network.
  • Reagent strengths (js/reagent_conc.js): the commercial concentrations of common reagents (for example HCl 37 %, HNO₃ 68–70 %, H₂SO₄ 95–98 %, H₂O₂ 30–35 %) as given in supplier specifications and the CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics; what counts as concentrated or dilute is a working definition listed in that file.
  • PubChem (NCBI/NLM), public domain: names and structures looked up online.
  • IUPAC Digitized pKa Dataset v2.4 (J. W. Zheng and O. Lafontant-Joseph, doi:10.5281/zenodo.7236452), CC BY-NC 4.0: measured pKa values (data/pka.js, tools/data/iupac_pka/). Reproduced by permission of International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry. Non-commercial use only.
  • Hammett substituent constants (σm, σp, σp⁻) from C. Hansch, A. Leo and R. W. Taft, Chem. Rev. 1991, 91, 165: the pKa estimates for substituted phenols, benzoic acids and anilines.
